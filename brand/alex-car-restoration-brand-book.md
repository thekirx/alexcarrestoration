# Alex Car Restoration — Internal Brand Guide

**Research date:** 2026-08-28  
**Scope:** Public Facebook presence only. Research and brand capture; not a redesign.  
**Primary source:** [Alex Car Restoration on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100064902093722)

## Executive Summary

Alex Car Restoration's existing identity is built less around elaborate graphic design than around a repeatable proof system: recognizable classic vehicles, clean shop photography, exact model naming, transformation language, a compact `AleX Restoration` watermark, and visible evidence of awards and long-term enthusiast credibility.

The brand is visually black-and-white with a very small red accent. Its most distinctive assets are the circular `AleX` profile mark and the horizontal `AleX Restoration` watermark/plate treatment. Photography does most of the communication. The strongest finished-project images are clean, bright, and controlled, but still rooted in the actual workshop rather than a generic luxury setting.

The verbal system has two modes:

1. **Restoration storytelling:** polished English, model-specific, transformation-led, and focused on craftsmanship, precision, patience, heritage, and rebirth.
2. **Commercial/service posts:** short, direct English with a message/phone/link CTA, sometimes followed by location, financing information, and hashtags.

The eventual website should therefore feel **proven, enthusiast-led, precise, image-rich, workshop-authentic, confident, and award-backed**. It should not become a generic black-and-gold luxury-car site. The strongest web concept will make Alex Car Restoration's own vehicles and shop the visual system.

**Overall confidence: HIGH** for the core logo, black/white palette, watermark behavior, finished-project photography, named services, and award-led credibility. **MEDIUM** for typography substitutes and the relative importance of vehicle sales. **LOW/UNKNOWN** for separate mechanical, upholstery, fabrication, or parts-sourcing services.

## Logo

- **Structure:** black circular field with a thick white perimeter ring. The white `AleX` lettering cuts across the circle; the final `X` has a small red wedge/accent on its right edge.
- **Wordmark behavior:** `A` and `X` are visually emphasized by capitalization. The letters are bold, tightly composed, and intentionally intersect the circle.
- **Color:** black, white, and a small vivid red accent.
- **Current use:** Facebook profile image / avatar.

### Website substitutes

- **Logo:** use the supplied logo artwork only. Never reproduce it with a webfont.
- **Display / headings:** `Roboto Condensed` 700 is a close, commercially usable substitute for the compact automotive utility character. Use sparingly and with modest tracking. **Substitute only; not identified as the logo font.**
- **Body:** `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` or a system sans stack. This is intentionally neutral because the social evidence does not establish a branded body face.
- **Utility / labels:** `Roboto Condensed` 500–700 in uppercase with restrained letter spacing can echo the small `Restoration` lockup and workshop labeling.

Avoid introducing a high-fashion serif, techno display font, or exaggerated racing italic. None is supported by the sampled identity.

## Social Media Visual System

### Dominant content types

1. Finished classic-car and motorcycle sets.
2. Restoration outcome stories with model-specific captions.
3. Detailing, PPF-removal, and ceramic-coating reels.
4. Cars offered for sale, usually in multi-image catalog sets.
5. Awards, car-show appearances, and team/client gratitude.
6. Occasional hiring and workshop/community posts.

### Recurring composition

- A hero view is usually followed by evidence: front, front three-quarter, side/rear three-quarter, engine bay or trunk, cabin, and tight detail crops.
- Front-on symmetry is common for iconic vehicles and the current cover image.
- Three-quarter views establish silhouette and paint finish.
- Detail images isolate badges, controls, leather stitching, spare wheels, engines, and paint/body surfaces with shallow depth of field when the shoot is more polished.
- Workshop context is not hidden. Polished concrete, lifts, shutters, other classic vehicles, equipment, and trophy cabinets repeatedly appear.
- People enter primarily as proof: owners, team members, presenters, award recipients, photographers, and show attendees. Cars remain dominant.

### Image treatment and overlays

- Most sampled images are photographic rather than poster-like.
- Large promotional headline overlays are not a dominant system. Facebook's automated “image of text” label is often reacting to the small watermark or vehicle lettering, not a designed poster.
- The most reliable brand treatment is a **small white `AleX Restoration` watermark at bottom center**.
- Borders and decorative frames are not a recurring signature in the sampled material.
- Polished projects use bright, controlled lighting and clean color; event images remain documentary.

### Aspect ratios and post size

- Finished stills commonly appear as square/social crops or landscape 3:2/4:3 images.
- Reels/service videos are commonly vertical 9:16.
- Multi-image posts are the norm for projects: sampled sets were about 7–10 images, with the Vespa feature at approximately 16.
- A recognizable Alex post is therefore not a single glamour image; it is a compact evidence set with a clear hero, multiple angles, details, and a bottom-center watermark.

## Photography Direction

All future site photography must be selected from Alex Car Restoration's own public social posts. Cropping, resizing, optimization, modest color balancing, and legibility gradients are acceptable; fabrication or substitution is not.

### Hero Image

Select a wide, high-resolution finished vehicle image in the Alex shop. Favor front-on symmetry or a calm three-quarter stance, visible shop context, clean reflections, and enough negative space for copy. The current cover and Datsun shop hero are the clearest patterns. Keep the existing watermark only if the crop does not duplicate a separately displayed logo.

### Featured Restoration

Select a confirmed restoration post—not merely a car-for-sale set. Lead with the finished three-quarter or front view; follow with engine/cabin/detail images from the same post. Best confirmed source sets are the Datsun 240Z, Mercedes 280SL, Vespa 50S, and Toyota Land Cruiser.

### Project Detail

Use close crops of badges, switches, stitching, chrome, wheel/tire assemblies, engine components, panel gaps, and paint finish. The Vespa set is the strongest observed example of this editorial detail language.

### Process Image

Select an image or frame that visibly proves work: disassembly, PPF removal, surface condition, bodywork, paint preparation, reassembly, or hands/tools in use. Do not repurpose a finished glamour image and label it “process.” The GT3 PPF-removal reel is a verified process source; the full Datsun and Vespa sets should be checked for additional in-progress frames before layout.

### Before / After

A search result labeled `AFTER— at Alex Car Restoration` and several transformation captions were found, but a clearly verified, paired before/after still set was not established in the sampled archive. **Status: UNKNOWN / requires manual source pairing.** Do not manufacture a comparison from unrelated images or mismatched angles.

### Shop / Craftsmanship

Choose wide workshop views where multiple classics, lifts, shutters, equipment, or staff establish that the work is real. The Datsun hero and current cover provide strong environmental context. Pair these with genuine task imagery if available.

### Awards / Credibility

Use trophy-lift, award-group, and show-floor images from the featured awards post. These are candid, busy, and documentary; preserve that character. The Vespa image with the trophy cabinet is a useful bridge between project and proof.

## Voice & Copy

### Language balance

- **Captions:** predominantly English in the sampled business posts.
- **Replies/community interaction:** brief, courteous English with Filipino honorifics such as `Sir` and `po`; occasional Filipino/Taglish is natural.
- **Confidence:** HIGH for English-dominant captions; MEDIUM for the overall Taglish ratio because not every historical caption was sampled.

### Voice attributes

- Model-specific and enthusiast-aware.
- Proud of transformation and finish.
- Craftsmanship- and precision-focused.
- Heritage-conscious without being academic.
- Commercially direct when selling or booking.
- Award-backed and community-facing.
- More personal than corporate, especially in thanks to the team, sponsors, and clients.

### Recurring verbal patterns

- Begin with the vehicle/model and the work performed.
- Frame restoration as a return, rebirth, or preservation of an icon.
- Name tangible work: frame-off/frame-on restoration, bodywork, oven-baked painting, detailing, paint correction, ceramic coating, PPF removal.
- Connect process to values: patience, precision, craftsmanship, heritage, authenticity, factory-fresh presentation.
- Close with one direct action: message, click the link in bio, book a restoration, or call.
- Add address/phone and a compact set of branded/model/service hashtags on longer posts.

### Caption length

- Restoration stories: two to four short paragraphs plus CTA/contact/hashtags.
- Service reels: often one-line model + service labels.
- Sales posts: short title, inquiry CTA, condition qualifier, then vehicle list or a brief collector-oriented description.

### Do

- Name the exact car and exact service.
- Describe visible, supportable work.
- Let outcomes and awards carry confidence.
- Use transformation language in moderation; the source voice genuinely uses “reborn,” “timeless,” and “restored.”
- Keep CTAs practical: `Book your restoration`, `Send us a message`, `Click the link in bio`, or phone/address details.
- Preserve the courteous, enthusiast-to-enthusiast feel.

### Don't

- Do not write anonymous “luxury automotive” prose that could belong to any shop.
- Do not make unsupported superlative claims such as world-class, unmatched, flawless, or the Philippines' best.
- Do not imply every photographed for-sale vehicle was restored by Alex.
- Do not invent provenance, rarity, originality, concours status, performance, or award history.
- Do not force Taglish into polished project copy; use it naturally in short community-facing moments only.
- Do not overwhelm copy with slogans when a model, process, and proof point would be stronger.
